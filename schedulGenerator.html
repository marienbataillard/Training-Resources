Five-minute schedule challenge

  1. Read the group. Note the number of families, total guests, ages, and celebrations.
  2. Plan the flow. Set a start, hut order, transition time, and a realistic finish.
  3. Adapt for guests. Include time or support for children, older guests, and anyone who may need a slower pace.
  4. Keep everyone together. Decide where the group waits, who leads and sweeps, and how you will regroup.
  5. Share the plan. Explain your schedule and one adjustment you made for this specific group.

Trainer tip: Use your real tour duration, hut sequence, and operating constraints when evaluating the schedule. The generator creates the guest scenario; it does not invent tour timings.

Three-minute hut presentation

Choose a hut and present it as if your training group were guests on tour.

Suggested structure

  1. Introduce the hut: name it and invite guests to look at a feature.
  2. Bring the structure to life: describe what it is made from, how it is built, or a detail guests can see.
  3. Explain its purpose: connect the hut to daily life, community, or cultural practice.
  4. Use five native terms: say each clearly and explain its meaning in context.
  5. Engage the guests: ask a question, invite them to notice something, or connect the story to their experience.
  6. Vary your delivery: change pace and tone to emphasize key moments, while keeping your words clear.

Trainer looks for

  • Five accurate native terms, explained naturally.
  • Accurate facts about the hut, its structure, and purpose.
  • Clear, audible speech and an easy-to-follow explanation.
  • Engagement: does the speaker make the hut interesting?
  • Cultural connection: do guests learn why the hut matters?
  • Vocal variety: does tone support the story rather than stay flat?
Practice rule: Stop at three minutes. Give feedback on one strength and one specific next step.

Group control: “Keep the group together”

Turn volume, clarity, and shared understanding into a short, observable practice challenge.

Challenge setup

One trainee gives a 60–90 second instruction for moving the guests from one hut to the next. The trainer quietly assigns a few guests a role: one is distracted, one is walking slowly, one cannot hear well, and one thinks the group is going to a different hut.

The trainee must get everyone’s attention, give clear directions, and confirm the group is ready before moving.

Look and listen for

  • Volume: can guests at the back hear without the speaker shouting?
  • Clarity: are the destination, route, and next action specific?
  • Shared understanding: does the trainee check that everyone is ready?
  • Positioning: can the guide see the group and keep the path clear?
  • Group awareness: do they notice who is missing, confused, or falling behind?

Useful instruction pattern

Get attention → say where to go → give one simple action → check readiness → move → count and regroup.

Example: “Everyone, please gather beside the entrance. We’re walking together to the next hut along this path. Watch your step, and let me know if you need a hand. Is everyone ready? Great—let’s go. I’ll count us once we arrive.” Adapt the wording to your tour and local protocols.
